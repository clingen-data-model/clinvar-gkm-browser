## 3. Use cases and workflows he emphasises

1. **Gene-first exploration** for curators and VCEPs: gene list, then gene page with its classification spectrum, condition breakdown and submitter breakdown.
   - Recruiting or contacting labs: find the top submitters in a gene, and read their classification mix to tell diagnostic testers from exome/genome incidental submitters (11:46–12:16).
   - Advice: start from the **gene, not the condition** (15:10).
2. **Narrowing to an actionable variant list.** Stack filters (★ criteria provided + clinical testing + clinically significant conflict) to go from 2,106 to 27 RYR1 variants. Click through to the list, then **Download table as spreadsheet** to annotate offline (5:08, 9:15–9:45).
3. **Conflict triage within a gene.** Use the significance × significance matrix and click a cell, such as P vs B, to get the variants. Restricting both axes to clinical labs changes the picture a lot: 11 P-vs-B pairs drop to 2 for LDLR (23:41–24:43).
4. **Lab-centred conflict review**, which takes the most screen time (25:14–31:13):
   - one lab against all others, as a matrix plus a per-opponent summary table;
   - one lab against a chosen lab (LMM vs Ambry);
   - **a lab against itself**, a self-audit for internally inconsistent submissions;
   - **a lab against expert panels only**, by setting the other side's minimum review status to ★★★;
   - **an expert panel against everyone else**, or against ★ clinical submitters only, to find out-of-date or alarming disagreements (ENIGMA benign vs a lab's pathogenic).
5. **Cross-gene comparison and normalisation.** This is done outside the tool. He exports counts and computes classification proportions per gene, the share of single- vs multi-submitter variants, and the conflict rate as a share of multi-submitted variants and of all variants. Point made: raw conflict counts mislead; BRCA1/2 have many conflicts but only 2–3% conflicting, while BTD has 42% (19:26–22:09).
6. **ClinVar-wide trends**: collection method over time, and submissions by country (31:23–33:24).

**Caveats and tips given:**
- The data are only ClinVar's, and the release date is always shown (1:20).
- Classification counts are variant × distinct-term counts, so they don't sum to the total (6:42).
- The same disease appears under several condition names because of different ontologies; condition views undercount (11:00, 14:51).
- "not specified" and "not provided" are the most common conditions, mostly on VUS, LB and B (12:47).
- Single-submitter idiosyncratic conditions exist (13:18).
- The "synonymous" and "confidence" conflict types are not conflicts in ClinVar's own sense (17:28–17:58).
- A variant can fall in several conflict columns (on-page note).
- Self-conflicts may be intentional when they are for different conditions (27:48).
- Unfiltered conflict rates can be inflated by old literature or research submissions; filter by submitter type (22:39).

## 4. Compared with our inventory (`docs/features.md`, `docs/research/clinvar-miner-and-landscape.md`)

### Shown in the video but missing from, or underspecified in, our inventory

1. **Comparing a submitter with itself.** On screen at 27:50: the head-to-head page with the same lab on both sides. Our inventory covers head-to-head but not self-comparison as a use case. It needs either `id1 == id2` support or a dedicated "internal consistency" view, and a way to tell apart self-conflicts on the same condition from those on different conditions.
2. **Submitter vs expert panels (and vs a review-status tier generally).** Our filters table lists `min_stars2`, but the video presents this as a headline workflow: "my lab vs expert panels" and "expert panel vs ★ clinical labs". It is worth naming in the UI as a preset, not just leaving as a filter.
3. **Named conflict categories.** The 2019 UI labels the columns: synonymous, confidence (B vs LB, P vs LP), benign/LB vs VUS, category (ACMG term vs non-ACMG term such as risk factor or drug response), and clinically significant. Our inventory says only "levels 1–5". We should keep the human-readable category names and the "e.g." examples in column headers (on screen), and the note that a variant can count in several columns.
4. **Overview column arithmetic** (single submission + ≥2 without conflict + any conflict = total). The presenter needed a slide to explain it. This argues for showing the derived totals and percentages ourselves (see 6).
5. **Classification-count semantics** (variant × distinct term; the sum exceeds the total). This is on-page text in ClinVar Miner. We should keep an explicit note, or offer "variants" vs "submissions" counting modes.
6. **Proportions and normalised rates.** The tool had none; he computed them by hand: the % breakdown of classifications per gene, % single- vs multi-submitter variants, and the conflict rate as a share of multi-submitted variants and of all variants. He also built a cross-gene 100% stacked bar chart. These were his main analytic points and are **not** ClinVar Miner features. They are a clear gap we can fill: percentage columns or toggles on the gene, condition and submitter conflict tables, and a "compare genes" view.
7. **The submitter profile line** ("Location: Australia — Primary collection method: curation") is on screen on conflict-by-submitter pages too, not only the by-submitter page. Minor.
8. **"See also" gene-combination links** on gene pages (LDLR → "LDLR, MIR6886", "DOCK6, LDLR", "LDLR, SMARCA4"). These come from multi-gene variants being listed as gene combinations, and our inventory doesn't mention them. We should decide how variants spanning several genes are counted under a single gene.
9. **"Search this table"** client-side filter box on every table (on screen everywhere). Not in our inventory. It's cheap and curators expect it.
10. **"Coded as" cross-ontology IDs** on condition pages (MONDO, OMIM, Orphanet, HP, UMLS, SNOMED). Our research doc mentions tagline links; the video shows them doing real work, letting a user see that near-duplicate condition names are the same disease. That supports our plan to key by MedGen and roll up by MONDO.
11. **Per-side filter labelling.** The labels change with context: "Submission 1/2", "Y axis/X axis", "the submission from <lab> / the other submission", and "Submission 1 (benign) / Submission 2 (pathogenic)" on the pair drill-down. Clear naming of each side is a UX detail worth copying.
12. **Variant lists show only HGVS + dbSNP** (on screen, 9:47 and 25:11). The video never opens a variant page; he says the hyperlink goes on to ClinVar. That argues for richer variant-list columns in ours: the classifications in conflict, submitters, stars, last evaluated.
13. **The submissions-by-method page had a line chart over time** (2013–2019), which our research doc notes was removed in 2024. The presenter used it to show ClinVar's growth.

### What the video suggests matters most in our inventory

- **Conflicts by submitter**: the one-vs-all matrix, the submitter-to-submitter summary, head-to-head and the pair drill-down. This takes about 6 of 35 minutes and has the most use cases (self, other lab, expert panel, panel vs labs). It matches our note "the most-used feature by labs."
- **Per-side review-status and collection-method filters** on conflict pages. Every conflict workflow he showed depended on them (clinical-lab-only, expert-panel-only, ★-and-above).
- **The gene page's three tables** (classification overview, condition × classification, submitter × classification), and **variants in conflict by gene** with its significance × significance matrix and clickable cells.
- **Download table as spreadsheet**, singled out as "always" available and the hand-off point for curation work. Our M3 streamed export of the full list is the right upgrade.
- **The release date / ClinVar version shown on every page.**
- **The gene-first approach over condition-first**, because condition names are fragmented. This supports MONDO roll-up (or MedGen grouping) as the fix, and suggests the gene path should be the default entry point.

### Shown but low priority

- The country choropleth got about 30 seconds, and he noted ClinVar has a similar map. This agrees with our plan to drop it in favour of a table.
- Gene type and antisense controls appear on every page but were never used or mentioned.
